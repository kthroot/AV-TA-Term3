### Part B - identify system

To start this out I think 3 things come out as rather significant looking at the 3 graphs, first the extra gain, second the lag, and 3rd
the machine backlash which arises due to decreasing and increasing (or reversing), well all of these 3 can easily be conflicted with one another
so the valid way to move forward is to check the arithmatic presence of each. 

What I can confirm definitely is that the actuator does have a gain in its slop as compared to the corresponding the commanded points, this comes 
out as 1.3 based on a functional calculation, m = v(output)/u(commanded) where v(output)= d(ymeasured)/dt this comes out to be ~1.3 for all cases, for 
example at the point of the platue in the deadband graph v(output) is 19.5 and the commanded is 15 hence the gain here becomes 1.3

there is a lag and towards the point of platue there is some more lag between at 3 where the steering wheel keeps moving for another 0.2 seconds

next was try to measure this mechanical cost which came out to be 0.06seconds. the 0.24 seconds decomposition observed in the velocity is essentially

What I mainly spent time doing was determining whether the lag was seperate from the mechanical cost because the model simply was not sufficing, hence it

### Part C - control design choices

My control design is based on position error {double error = target - measured;} 

The proportional term provides the primary response to position error. The integral term removes the position error, which is important because 
the identified actuator has approximately 2.5 degrees of mechanical cost. I measured the derivative term from the measured position rather than
the from the error since as per the data the target angles can change very suddenly in the case of reversals or jumps up and down.

As for the initial PID gains, i had actually decided to run and check experimentally for what worked but I was running out of time so I provided 
the code to a LLM and asked it to optimise the gains, hence producing the result you see. 
The commanded velocity is limited to +/- 15 deg/s since it is with in the range used by actuator in the tests.
The anti windup is done by the integral since it is bounded down hence it will not push the actuator when it is already over saturated.
